🚀 Jensen Huang: "Compute Is Revenue" — Nvidia Just Rewrote the Rules of the AI Trade

🎥 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fz4mLwW5Rx0

Fresh off Nvidia's blowout $96B quarter, Jensen Huang sat down with Jim Cramer on Mad Money and delivered the most consequential CEO interview of the year. In four words — "compute is revenue" — he redrew the map for hyperscalers, sovereign nations, and every investor still asking whether AI capex is a bubble.

Here's what he actually said:

⸻

Key Takeaways

1️⃣ "Compute Is Revenue" — Tokens Are Now Profitable 💰
"AI has reached its inflection point. It's doing useful work. Its tokens are productive and profitable. Now, compute is revenue." Huang collapsed the entire bull case into four words and repeated it to Cramer — chips no longer sell a promise, they mint dollar-generating tokens. That reframing is why hyperscalers keep tripling capex and why he thinks the market is still under-modeling Nvidia's economic engine.

2️⃣ 70% Growth Guide Blows Away the Street 📈
Huang forecast approximately 70% year-over-year revenue growth for fiscal 2028 — a supply-constrained outlook against a Wall Street consensus closer to 45%. "Demand is super strong and, incredibly, it's accelerating," he told Cramer, adding "to be able to grow continuously and now to accelerate our growth is pretty extraordinary." Q2 already printed a record $96.2B, up 106% year over year.

3️⃣ A "Golden Age" of Frontier Labs 🌅
"This time last year, one lab alone was driving the buildout. Today, we have a golden age of new AI labs and startups, multiple frontier labs scaling in parallel, a thriving open-model ecosystem and physical AI coming online — with strong momentum across the U.S." Huang's message: Nvidia's demand base has diversified far beyond a single hyperscaler bet. That structural shift is what backs the aggressive forward guide.

4️⃣ Vera Rubin Is Locked In — Annual Cadence Holds 🔁
"Vera Rubin is our next-generation product…we're rolling it out." Huang told Cramer Blackwell is still ramping while the next-gen platform is already teed up for its ship window, keeping Nvidia's one-architecture-per-year rhythm intact. Every cycle drops inference token cost by roughly an order of magnitude — a curve custom-silicon competitors have no way to shadow.

5️⃣ Sovereign AI Is No Longer Optional 🌍
"Every single country needs to build infrastructure so that you can support your own local economy." Huang doubled down on his G20 message: AI is infrastructure like water, roads, electricity or the internet, and Nvidia is "the only computing platform that is in every single cloud, on-prem, at the edge, in sovereign AI clouds, in neo-clouds." That ubiquity is why the outlook doesn't rely on any one region.

6️⃣ Physical AI Is Now a Revenue Line 🤖
"The age of generalist robotics is here." Nvidia unveiled Isaac GR00T N1.6 (its open humanoid reference on Jetson Thor) and NVIDIA Halos — the industry's first full-stack safety system for physical AI — during earnings week, and Huang told Cramer companies will build their robotics fleets on the Nvidia stack. Robotics moves from science project to booked capex line, opening a genuinely new billion-dollar market.

7️⃣ $400 Billion of VC Is Chasing This Platform Shift 💸
"In the last six months, $400 billion of venture capital went into startups because of AI." Huang used the number to answer any lingering "is this a bubble?" question, framing the moment as a once-in-a-generation platform shift on par with the arrival of the PC or the smartphone. It's why he thinks the AI capex cycle is closer to inning two than the ninth.

8️⃣ The Full-Stack "AI Factory" Moat 🏭
"Nvidia is the only computing platform that is a full-stack AI factory." From energy at the bottom to chips, systems, networking, CUDA, models, and applications on top, Huang told Cramer no rival covers every layer of what he calls the five-layer cake. That's his answer to every custom-silicon threat: competitors ship a chip, Nvidia ships a factory.

9️⃣ Nvidia Is Buying Back Its Own Stock — Hard 💵
"We bought back 60% last quarter and will keep buying with next year's free cash flow." Asked about the after-hours selloff, Huang effectively said Nvidia would take the other side of it — an unusually direct on-air capital allocation signal from a CEO the day of earnings. The message: management thinks the stock is still mispriced despite the 106% growth print.

🔟 China Is a Bonus, Not the Base Case 🇨🇳
Huang confirmed that H20 shipments to China made up less than 1% of data center revenue in the quarter and that the FY28 outlook assumes zero data-center compute revenue from China. "We're not counting on China" is the core message — meaning any thaw in export controls is pure upside. It flips the "China risk" narrative that has weighed on NVDA all year into an unpriced call option.

⸻

Final Thought

💡 Huang used his post-earnings victory lap not to celebrate the $96B print but to redraw the AI trade itself: this is no longer a chip cycle, it's a compute-as-revenue economy — with sovereign nations, humanoid robots, and open-source labs all queued up as customers. If FY28's 70% guide holds, this Mad Money sit-down will be remembered as the moment "AI factory" replaced "chip" as the unit of analysis for the entire market.
