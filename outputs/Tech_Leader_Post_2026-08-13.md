## Formatted X Post

🚀 Sam Altman: OpenAI Is About to Build a "Genie That Grants Any Wish" — And Compute Is the New Oil

🎥 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XDB5beon4DY

Sam Altman just sat down with Patrick O'Shaughnessy on Invest Like the Best and dropped what might be the most unfiltered state-of-AI address of the year. He talks GPT-5.6, "Universal Basic Compute," why he thinks we're already inside the singularity — and where OpenAI is quietly betting the company.

Here's what he actually said:

⸻

Key Takeaways

1️⃣ "We Are About to Build a Genie" 🧞
"I think this will be the greatest technological achievement in human history so far." Altman argues OpenAI is on the cusp of a near-AGI system able to grant nearly any human wish. The framing is deliberate — the challenge isn't building the genie, it's making sure no single company or government ends up owning the lamp.

2️⃣ GPT-5.6 Is Already "Very AGI-Like" 🧠
Altman says the newest model is already brushing up against AGI territory, and that OpenAI briefed US lawmakers on it before wide release. The point isn't the label — it's that the capability jump between versions is now happening in months, not years, and internal expectations for the next 12 months are the most aggressive they've ever set.

3️⃣ Universal Basic Compute 💡
Forget UBI — Altman is floating "Universal Basic Compute": every person on Earth gets a baseline allocation of AI capability as a fundamental resource. If intelligence becomes the primary input to everything, then who gets access to it becomes the defining equity question of the decade. This is Altman staking OpenAI's ideological ground.

4️⃣ A Gigawatt Every Week ⚡
OpenAI's plan: bring one full gigawatt of new compute capacity online every single week. Altman defended the early decision to "buy compute at a scale nobody thought was rational" and doubled down — demand, he insists, is effectively limitless. This is why Stargate, the Jalapeno chip, and the fresh AWS Bedrock deal all matter.

5️⃣ "We Are Now, Like, In the Singularity" 🌀
On the companion Relentless podcast, Altman said it plainly: the transition isn't coming — it's happening. He points to persistent AI agents as the third phase of the AI era (after chatbots and coding assistants), and argues most people haven't yet noticed the ground has already shifted under them.

6️⃣ The Real Risk Isn't Skynet — It's Regulatory Capture 🏛️
Altman's sharpest warning: the biggest danger isn't a runaway model, it's a small number of incumbents locking in the future under the banner of "AI safety." He's telegraphing a direct pushback against rivals lobbying Washington for licensing regimes that would freeze the frontier in place.

7️⃣ Build Robots — Or Humans Become Actuators 🤖
"We need to build robots so that humans are not reduced to actuators of cloud intelligence." In other words: if AI does all the thinking and humans do all the physical labor for it, we've inverted the deal. Robotics, for Altman, is now a human-agency problem, not just an engineering one.

8️⃣ The Focus Bet: Kill the Portfolio, Own the Platform 🎯
Altman explained why OpenAI abandoned its sprawling product surface area to concentrate everything on the core intelligence platform. Fewer bets, deeper moats. "The past year has been really tough, and it's partly my fault. But the next 12 months could be our best ever."

9️⃣ Open-Source and Distillation Don't Scare Him 🔓
On Kimi, distilled clones, and the Hugging Face breach, Altman was almost dismissive. Distillation copies today's frontier — it can't chase a frontier that's moving this fast. His confidence: the moat isn't the weights, it's the compute, the data flywheel, and the pace.

🔟 Raising Kids Who've Never Known Scarcity of Intelligence 👶
The most human moment of the interview: Altman on what it's like to raise children who will grow up never knowing a world without abundant AI. It reframes the whole conversation — this isn't a product launch, it's a generational handoff.

⸻

Final Thought

💡 Altman's message is bigger than any single model release: the frontier is moving faster than the discourse, compute is becoming the geopolitical resource of the century, and the fight that actually matters is over distribution — who gets access to the genie. If he's even half right, the next 12 months reshape not just the AI industry, but the shape of work, agency, and power itself.
