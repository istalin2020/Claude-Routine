## Formatted X Post

🚀 Sam Altman: "We Are About to Build a Genie That Can Grant Any Wish" — And Power, Not the Wish, Is the Real Problem

🎥 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XDB5beon4DY

Sam Altman just sat down for the most candid interview of his year — a two-hour tour through OpenAI's compute empire, the coming robotics moment, and the one thing that actually keeps him up at night. This isn't hype. It's a decel-pill from the man building the accelerator.

Here's what he actually said:

⸻

Key Takeaways

1️⃣ The "Magic Lamp" Is Coming — And It Scares Him 🧞
"The concentration of power with AI is a terrifying thing. I don't think anyone should want to live in a world of AI overlords or a company that is the rough equivalent of that." Altman reframed OpenAI's mission as building "a genie that can grant any wish" — but insisted the wish isn't the danger. The distribution of who owns the lamp is the defining challenge of the decade.

2️⃣ He's Ready to Decelerate 🛑
"We may have to pace the rate of AI development to give ourselves enough time for society to harden around some of these new capability levels." Coming from the CEO who spent three years pushing the accelerator to the floor, this is a startling reversal. TechCrunch's headline said it plainly: "Sam Altman is ready to decelerate."

3️⃣ A Gigawatt a Week 🏗️
OpenAI's build-out plan is now measured in gigawatts of new compute capacity per week — a rate no industry has ever sustained. "We started calling it clouds. We started calling it chip fab… everyone's like, you're totally crazy. This is impossible. No industry has ever moved like this. This is reckless." He wore the "reckless" label like a badge.

4️⃣ Meet "Jalapeno" — OpenAI's Own Silicon 🌶️
Altman confirmed the company's in-house chip program is real, code-named Jalapeno, and it's the linchpin of the gigawatt-a-week plan. It signals OpenAI is no longer content to be Nvidia's biggest customer — it wants to own the stack. The move puts it on a collision course with its own suppliers.

5️⃣ GPT-5.6 Is "Very AGI-like" — But Still Missing Something 🧠
Altman said the current flagship, GPT-5.6 Sol, is "approaching very AGI-like" behavior, yet he refused to call it AGI. "It's still missing something." He hinted the next model will "shock the world" inside the next 12 months — a rare, specific commitment from a CEO usually careful with dates.

6️⃣ The "ChatGPT Moment" for Robotics Is 2–3 Years Away 🤖
"Not watching a video of a robot dog, but ordinary people being able to personally input commands and watch a robot complete a task with their own eyes." Altman put a clock on it: within two to three years, physical AI gets its viral, mind-blowing consumer moment. That's a direct shot at Tesla, Figure, and every humanoid startup racing to be first.

7️⃣ Personal Agents Will See Everything on Your Screen 👁️
The third phase of AI — after chatbots and coding copilots — is persistent, screen-aware agents that live alongside you all day. "The core bottleneck is compute." He described a near-future where your AI watches every pixel, remembers every context, and acts on your behalf. Privacy hawks are going to have a field day.

8️⃣ No "Jobs Apocalypse" — But No 4-Hour Workweek Either 💼
Altman explicitly walked back his own earlier doom talk: "I'm not a jobs doomsayer." What worries him instead is the concentration of power hiding behind the "AI safety" label. He was equally blunt on the utopian side — don't expect AI to hand humanity a four-hour workweek. New capabilities create new work, not just less of the old.

9️⃣ Unfazed by Open Source and Distillation 🌊
Asked about Kimi, DeepSeek, and the flood of open-source distillations chasing OpenAI's frontier, Altman shrugged. Distillation only works if there's a frontier to distill from — and OpenAI plans to keep the gap wide. "The next 12 months will be our best ever."

🔟 Raising Kids in a Post-Scarcity Mind 👶
The most human moment came when Altman talked about being a new father. His kids will grow up "never knowing a world without abundant intelligence" — a phrase that landed harder than any technical claim in the episode. It reframed the entire conversation from "product roadmap" to "generational transition."

⸻

Final Thought

💡 Two things can be true at once: OpenAI is pushing harder than any company in history — a gigawatt a week, custom silicon, agents that watch your screen — and its CEO is publicly asking the world to slow down. The tell isn't in the tech; it's in the tone. When the man building the genie starts warning about who gets to hold the lamp, it's worth listening.
