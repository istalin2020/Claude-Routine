🚀 Jensen Huang: "We're Practically at AGI" — Nvidia's CEO Pitches AI as National Infrastructure at G20 and Puts a $50–60B Price Tag on the Future

🎥 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EFY-qjUZPkc

In a fireside chat with U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick at the G20 Innovation Ministerial in Chapel Hill, Jensen Huang did something no Nvidia CEO has done before: he treated AI as a nation-building question, not a product question. Between AGI concessions, gigawatt price tags, and a full-frontal challenge to Bill Gates, this may be the most consequential Huang appearance of 2026.

Here's what he actually said:

⸻

Key Takeaways

1️⃣ "We're Practically at AGI Today" 🤖
"In the next couple of years, we are going to achieve essentially what people call AGI… we're practically there today." Coming from the man who supplies the compute for every frontier model on Earth, that is the strongest AGI concession Huang has ever made on a public stage. He said it in front of G20 innovation ministers — which turns a private-lab boast into a global policy trigger.

2️⃣ AI Is Infrastructure — Not A Gadget 🏗️
"In the end, what every single country will have to recognize is that AI is infrastructure — just as it's water, roads, electricity, the internet." Huang told ministers they should stop treating AI like a consumer app and start treating it like a national utility. It's Nvidia arguing, in front of the U.S. Commerce Secretary and 19 other governments, that AI factories belong in the same category as power plants and highways.

3️⃣ $50–60 Billion Per Gigawatt — And The World Needs 100 Of Them 💸
"One gigawatt is about $50, $60 billion. We're building — between now and the end of the decade — 100 gigawatts, because we want to produce intelligence for the world." Multiply it out: that's a $5–6 trillion AI-factory buildout Huang is telling G20 ministers to plan around. It reframes hyperscaler capex from a company story into a sovereign policy target.

4️⃣ "The Worst Outcome Is Being Left Behind" ⚠️
"The single worst outcome for any country is being left behind." Huang used his G20 platform to warn — bluntly — that ministers who over-index on AI risk debates instead of AI adoption will pay for it with lost growth for decades. Between the lines, it's the case against European-style AI Act regulation delivered directly to the ministers writing similar bills at home.

5️⃣ The Five-Layer AI Cake — And Nvidia Wants The Whole Thing 🎂
Huang described AI as a "five-layer cake" — energy, chips, data-center infrastructure, models, then data and applications — and argued the U.S. is trying to lead every layer. Nvidia isn't just the chip company anymore; Huang publicly positioned it as a stack company competing on every rung from power grids to inference APIs. That is a strategic map for the next decade of Nvidia acquisitions, partnerships, and investments.

6️⃣ AI Will Be A Net Job Creator — Directly Rebutting Bill Gates 🛠️
Days earlier on Fox Business, Huang went at Bill Gates directly: "I love the heck out of Bill, but I don't see what he sees." His G20 pitch continued that argument — "hundreds of thousands of jobs are being created as we speak" in chip plants, packaging, AI factories, and the trades. Huang is now the loudest voice in Silicon Valley telling electricians, plumbers, and carpenters that the AI buildout is theirs to win, not lose.

7️⃣ Every Country Needs Its Own Sovereign AI 🌍
"Every single country needs to build infrastructure so you can support your own local economy." Huang has been pushing sovereign AI for two years — this was the moment he pitched it to a G20 ministerial that then endorsed it. The consensus statement out of Chapel Hill effectively backed his framing, giving Nvidia a running start on selling full-stack AI factories to every G20 capital.

8️⃣ Fear-Forward Debates Are Skewing Policy 🗳️
Huang warned that public debates about "theoretical harm" from AI are pushing governments toward policies that trade real economic growth for imaginary safety. It's an unusually blunt attack on the regulate-first camp — delivered inside a G20 process — and it landed alongside a Business Standard confirmation that the ministerial produced consensus support for "light-touch" AI rules, exactly the outcome Huang was arguing for.

9️⃣ AI Factories Are The New Semiconductor Fabs 🏭
Huang re-cast Nvidia's product not as GPUs but as "AI factories" — full-stack systems (chips + networking + power + software) that manufacture intelligence at industrial scale. He told ministers that the biggest opportunity of the next decade isn't building better chatbots, it's building the factories that produce them at gigawatt scale. Every country, in his framing, will need at least one.

🔟 A Parade Of Moguls — And Nvidia Setting The Agenda 👑
Lutnick's Chapel Hill lineup was extraordinary: Huang, Sam Altman, Tom Brown (Anthropic), Alex Karp (Palantir), Elon Musk virtually. But it was Huang who set the vocabulary the summit adopted — infrastructure, gigawatts, five-layer stack, sovereign AI. When the G20 Innovation Ministerial Consensus Statement dropped, it read like the Nvidia investor deck: AI as essential infrastructure, light-touch rules, adoption-first. That is a policy win most companies never achieve.
